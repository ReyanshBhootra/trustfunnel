The problem

The ATS stopped being a filter.

Generated applications pass keyword screening by design — that is what they are optimized for. The volume lands on a recruiter who now has to answer three questions per candidate, by hand, with nothing but a PDF and a video call to go on.

How it works

Not one clever trick. A funnel.

Three gated checkpoints. Each one has to clear before the next runs — so nobody evaluates the resume quality of a person who isn't real, and nobody scores the fit of a resume that doesn't hold up.

01
open ↗

Identity

Prove this is a real, specific, live human.
  • Face and government-ID baseline at signup
  • Unscripted reaction challenge mid-interview — face-swap tools break on spontaneous movement
  • Lip-sync and voice-pitch drift sampled across the call, not once
  • Same face or voice print appearing under other names
Identity Score → must clear to unlock Gate 2
fails here → held, Gates 2 and 3 never run

open ↗

Consistency

Confirm the application material is genuine and internally consistent.
  • Resume provenance — built over sessions, or pasted in as one finished block
  • Keystroke rhythm: human hesitation vs. flat machine timing
  • Titles, dates and companies against what's publicly verifiable
  • LinkedIn cross-check, including whether the profile itself looks real
Consistency Score → must clear to unlock Gate 3
fails here → stays ranked, flagged, fit never scored

Fit

Is this a good match, and have we seen them before?
  • Semantic match of verified experience against the posting — unverified claims are discounted, not trusted
  • Prior TrustFunnel history across companies: cleared clean, or flagged and why
  • Behavioral pattern — a focused search, or 200 generated applications

What the recruiter sees

One ranked queue. Nothing silently deleted.

The score decides the order, not the outcome. Candidates who fail identity stay visible in a held section instead of vanishing, and anyone mid-pipeline reads as in progress rather than scoring low for work that hasn't finished.
